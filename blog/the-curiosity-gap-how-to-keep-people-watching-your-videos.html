The Curiosity Gap: How to Keep People Watching Your Videos

Why do some videos make you think “I need to know what happens”?

Often, the answer is the curiosity gap.

A curiosity gap is created when you give viewers enough information to understand something interesting, but leave one important question unanswered.

That unanswered question becomes a reason to keep watching.

How Does a Curiosity Gap Work?

Imagine a video starting with:

“This coffee shop was losing customers every day. Then they changed one thing.”

You immediately have a question:

What did they change?

The video has opened a loop, and the answer has been delayed.

Compare that with:

“This coffee shop changed its menu and got more customers.”

You already know the result. There is much less reason to keep watching.

The difference is what you reveal and what you leave unresolved.

3 Simple Curiosity Gap Examples

The Mystery

“Nobody could figure out why this restaurant was always empty.”

You want to know why.

The Unexpected Result

“This $5 product outsold the $50 version.”

Now you want to know how.

The Teased Reveal

“We tested three coffee machines. The cheapest one was the winner.”

Which one?

The viewer has a specific question they want answered.

Curiosity Isn't Clickbait

A curiosity gap only works if you eventually close it.

If you promise an interesting answer and never deliver it, the viewer feels manipulated. A strong open loop creates anticipation and then gives the audience a satisfying payoff.

Think of it as a simple structure:

Open a question → Build toward the answer → Deliver the payoff

The goal isn't to hide information.

It's to make the viewer want the information before you give it to them.

The Simple Rule

When writing your next video script, ask:

“What question can I make the viewer want answered?”

If you can create that question in the first few seconds — and make the answer worth waiting for — you've already given your video a stronger reason to be watched.
